GAMEFROMZERO · DAY 79

🪙 Penney’s ante — you pick a length-3 pattern of heads and tails, then I pick one after seeing yours. We flip a fair coin until one appears, and whoever’s pattern lands first wins. Nothing here is simulated and nothing is a float: each pair is solved as an absorbing Markov chain over rationals with BigInt numerator and denominator, so “I win 2/3 of the time” is 2n/3n and not 0.6667 — and every answer is cross-checked against Conway’s leading-numbers algorithm, which shares no code with the chain. 🔴 Whatever you pick, I have a reply that wins — and the worst case for me is 2/3. Pick HHH and I take 7/8. There is no pattern you can choose that leaves me under a two-to-one favourite. 🔴 The intuition that decides it for most people is simply wrong: HHT, HTT, THH and TTH all wait exactly 8 flips on average, and yet THH beats HHT 3/4 of the time. Which pattern appears soonest on its own says nothing about which appears first when two are racing. 🔴 And the shape of the game changes with the length in a way I did not expect: at length 2 the second player’s best reply wins exactly 1/2 — no advantage exists at all — while at length 4 the relation becomes genuinely cyclic, with 42 triples where A beats B beats C beats A. At length 3 there are none.

Every pattern, and its best answer

1. The number people decide on says nothing

Ask anyone which pattern to pick and they reason about how long it takes to appear. That is a real quantity, it is computable exactly, and it is not what decides this game.

Four patterns wait exactly 8 flips. They are not interchangeable. THH beats HHT 3/4 of the time, and both wait 8. Waiting time is a property of one pattern on its own; winning is a property of the pair, and the two questions have different answers.

2. Why going second is worth so much

The mechanism is overlap. Your pattern has to appear somewhere in the stream, and the two flips before it are not free — they are part of the stream too. I choose a pattern that sits on the front of yours, so that most of the ways your pattern could begin have already completed mine.

Against HHH, the reply THH wins 7/8 for a reason you can say in one sentence: unless the very first three flips are HHH, a T must appear before your run completes — and that T, followed by the HH you need, is my pattern. The only surviving case is the 1/8 where you win immediately.

3. The length changes the game, and not gradually

At length 2 the best reply wins exactly 1/2: going second is worth nothing, and the game does not exist. At length 3 the second player is guaranteed at least 2/3 and the “beats” relation is a strict order — zero three-cycles. At length 4 the guaranteed edge drops to 9/14 and the relation becomes genuinely non-transitive, with 42 triples where A beats B beats C beats A. 🔴 I expected the cycles at length 3, because that is where this game is always demonstrated. They are not there.

What is exact here, and what a fraction cannot tell you

Every probability is a rational solved from the chain and confirmed by Conway. What that does not give you is variance: 2/3 is the probability of winning a single race, and short sessions will not look like it. The page reports the exact odds of one game, which is the quantity the choice actually turns on — not a claim about what any particular evening of coin-flipping will look like.

How it works

A race between two patterns is a Markov chain whose state is the longest suffix of the flips so far that is a prefix of either pattern. That is a finite set, the two patterns are absorbing, and the probability of reaching one before the other is a linear system. 

// state after one more flip
next = longestSuffixPrefix(state + c, a, b);

// and the system, solved over rationals rather than floats
x[i] = 1/2 * x[next(i,'H')] + 1/2 * x[next(i,'T')]

Conway’s algorithm gets the same answer from four numbers: for patterns X and Y, the leading number XY sets bit k when the last k characters of X equal the first k of Y. The odds that B beats A are then (AA − AB) : (BB − BA). No chain, no linear algebra — which is exactly why it is worth checking against.
